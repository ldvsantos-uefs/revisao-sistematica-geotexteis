We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled “Chemical Architecture and Weibull Reliability of Typha and Syagrus Natural Geotextiles for Soil Erosion Control” for consideration in Industrial Crops and Products.This study examines the potential of underutilized tropical species (Typha domingensis and Syagrus coronata) to serve as high-performance raw materials for the geotechnics industry. Moving beyond standard characterization, we propose a reliability-based framework that connects the specific chemical architecture of these fibers to their service life in soil bioengineering applications. Using a combination of 180-day field exposure data, microstructural characterization (SEM/XRD), and probabilistic modeling, we assess the mechanical reliability of these materials under real-world degradation conditions.We show that the lignin/cellulose (L/C) ratio acts as a robust predictor of degradation kinetics ($k$) and functional longevity; that moderate alkaline surface engineering (6% NaOH) optimizes the trade-off between structural integrity and ductility, extending the functional window to match vegetative establishment; and that Weibull reliability analysis ($P_{10}$) functions as a superior specification metric compared to traditional mean strength values, supporting risk-based design in soil stabilization.Our manuscript contributes to Industrial Crops and Products’ focus on the valorization of crops for industrial applications by offering an empirically grounded, reproducible framework for specifying natural fibers. The contribution is framed not merely as a biodegradation study, but as a proposal for "engineered durability" where the service life of the industrial product is treated as a parameter controlled by the crop's chemical architecture.We confirm that this work is original, not under review elsewhere, and that all authors meet the journal’s authorship criteria. The authors report no competing interests. Funding and data availability statements are provided in the submission files.Thank you for considering our submission. We look forward to the opportunity to contribute to the journal's ongoing dialogue on bio-based materials and sustainable industrial products.
